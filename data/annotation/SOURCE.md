# Third-party data — GPCRdb

The two files in this directory are **not** original to this study. They are
redistributed from **GPCRdb** (<https://gpcrdb.org>). The copyright on the rest of this
repository does not extend to them.

GPCRdb's [legal notice](https://docs.gpcrdb.org/legal_notice.html) licenses its **data**
under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) and its **source code**
under Apache 2.0. `gpcrdb_generic_numbers.tsv` is plainly data. `snakeplot_raw.svg` is a
diagram *generated by* GPCRdb's code, and the notice does not say which licence covers
generated output; it is redistributed here as data, with attribution either way. Worth a
note to GPCRdb before the figure is published if you want that settled.

| file | what it is | retrieved | from |
|---|---|---|---|
| `snakeplot_raw.svg` | residue layout of the GPR161 topology (snake) diagram | 2026-09-08 | the `<svg id="snakeplot">` element of <https://gpcrdb.org/protein/gp161_human/> |
| `gpcrdb_generic_numbers.tsv` | GPCRdb segment assignment and Ballesteros-Weinstein generic numbers per position | 2026-09-08 | GPCRdb residue service for `gp161_human` |

**Changes made.** `snakeplot_raw.svg` is committed unmodified; the figure code recolours
it at draw time (`scripts/style.py::snake_axes` reads only the circle coordinates, so
only the *layout* is GPCRdb's and the colours are this study's data).
`gpcrdb_generic_numbers.tsv` is a tabulation of the service response with one added
column, `our_bw`, reconciling the served Ballesteros-Weinstein number with this study's
numbering; `agrees` is True only where both are present and equal.

**Attribution.** Cite GPCRdb when reusing either file. Note that GPCRdb's citing page
still lists an older paper as its main reference while the site's front page points at
the 2025 one; the 2025 paper is the current release article:

> Taracena Herrera, L. P. *et al.* GPCRdb in 2025: adding odorant receptors, data mapper,
> structure similarity search and models of physiological ligand complexes.
> *Nucleic Acids Research* **53**, D425-D435 (2025). doi:10.1093/nar/gkae1065

and, for the generic numbering scheme itself, both the original scheme and GPCRdb's
implementation of it, which is what GPCRdb asks for:

> Ballesteros, J. A. & Weinstein, H. Integrated methods for the construction of
> three-dimensional models and computational probing of structure-function relations in
> G protein-coupled receptors. *Methods in Neurosciences* **25**, 366-428 (1995).

> Isberg, V. *et al.* Generic GPCR residue numbers — aligning topology maps while minding
> the gaps. *Trends in Pharmacological Sciences* **36**, 22-31 (2015).
> doi:10.1016/j.tips.2014.11.001
